What just happened

The hint faded on its own schedule

As you scrolled through the pinned stage above, <scroll-hint> read the same progressOf(heroWrap) value already driving the hero's fade-and-scale, and mapped it to its own opacity: 1 − progress × 6, clamped 0–1. It's fully gone by ~16% into the pin — long before the headline finishes fading — because its job (tell you the page scrolls) is done the instant you prove you already know that.

Use the toolbar toggle above to preview the prefers-reduced-motion: reduce state without changing your OS setting. Source

Page chrome above (toolbar, hero copy, these sections) is Tailwind utility classes end to end. The component's own internals — its label color, the drip-line animation, its default tokens — stay plain CSS inside its shadow root, unchanged from the vanilla implementation documented in scroll-hint-instructions.md, because Tailwind's generated stylesheet can't reach across a shadow boundary — that split is structural, not a style preference.
