design / toast · v2 — grammar-aligned

Toast

It fills.
It runs down.
Then it's gone.

One wash of colour across the whole pill, instead of four small parts competing for the same 40px. Its hue says what happened. How much of it is left says how long you have. Both are true of the same layer at once, because they are different properties of it — not two shapes fighting for one centre.

colour = state volume = time swipe to dismiss glyph on threshold only 6 rail positions

01

One layer, two honest jobs

Both signals are required — a toast that vanishes without warning is a broken promise, and one that doesn't say what happened isn't a notification. So neither gets dropped. They get put on properties that don't compete: hue for what happened, volume for how long you have. The colour fills the pill and retracts as the clock runs down. Every value below is taken from the grammar rather than invented — the system already codifies a quantity on a dark surface, in .rail.dark.

The volume is two layers of one hue, not one. Underneath, a low base tint covers the whole pill and never leaves — so the colour is still readable when the volume is nearly spent. On top, the volume itself retracts leftward, and its trailing edge is a 2.5rem feather rather than a cut. Nothing straight ever crosses a shape that has no straight sides, and because the feather scales down with the volume, there's no sliver or remnant left against the rounded cap at the end.

Where the green went

--ok has exactly one sanctioned use in the whole system — .act[data-state="success"], a solid fill on a button. A 320×48 green wash was roughly 120× that area with no relative anywhere on the site, which is precisely why it never read clearly. Success now lands on the exit circle: solid --ok, white check, the same colour the visitor already learned from every action button here. Failure mirrors it — .act[data-state="error"] is a white ground with an --err mark, and so is the failing circle.

One default size

Every toast opens at the same --toast-w-base of 20rem. Longer copy pushes past it, up to the rail's cap; shorter copy does not pull it in. A stack of three should read as one consistent object repeated, not a ragged column of three different widths — and the message always gets the full default measure whether or not it strictly needs it.

02

A glyph for the threshold, not the stay

The icon is the toast, before and after it is a toast. It scales up from nothing, becomes a pill, and at the end becomes an icon again and scales back to nothing. Three strict phases each way, and each one waits for the last to finish — the expansion doesn't start until the icon has actually landed. The glyph belongs to the two moments of crossing, never to the stay between them.

Which way it expands isn't the toast's decision — it falls out of the rail's alignment. Anchored right, the right edge stays pinned and the pill grows leftward; anchored left, it grows rightward; centred, it grows both ways, exactly like the pricing contact pill. Try it against the six positions in section 06.

Arrives

1. The bell scales 0 → 100 as a circle, 300ms. 2. Only then does it expand into the pill, 420ms, the mark retiring as it goes. 3. The clock arms. A bell always — the outcome isn't known yet, so the mark doesn't guess at one.

Leaves, on its own time

The arrival played backwards. 1. The pill shrinks to the circle, 420ms. 2. The outcome appears in it and holds, 340ms. 3. The icon scales 100 → 0, 300ms. It scales away — it does not fade, or the two thresholds stop reading as one gesture.

Fail is the one moment this component inverts — a solid white disc, ink mark, for that single closing beat. Same move the alert dialog reserves for a destructive default: the site raises its voice by flipping the ground it stands on, never by reaching for red.

03

Undo, in the fill

An actionable toast wears accent, and the volume retracting is the window in which the action still works. Press it and nothing new appears — the same pill swaps its tone, its line, and its fill, and resets to a short run before it leaves normally.

Delete with an undo window

Seven seconds of accent, retracting. Let it run out and the delete stands.

Action that isn't undo

Same mechanism, opening something instead of reversing it.

04

The same thing twice is not two things

Give a toast an id and a repeat won't stack a duplicate. The pill on screen refills to full, ticks a tabular ×n counter, and that's all.

Three at a time, maximum. A fourth expires the oldest early — through the same collapse-to-glyph exit as a natural timeout, since that is exactly what happened to it.

05

One pill for the whole request

Opens with the fill quietly sweeping — the same motion the skeleton loader already uses, so nothing new is invented to say "working." The volume holds full while there's no deadline yet to spend. When the promise settles, it switches from sweep to a full volume in the outcome's colour, and only then starts retracting.

Resolves

Sweep → full green wash → retracts over four seconds → gone.

Rejects

Sweep → full red wash → retracts over eight seconds, reason on the line.

06

Six corners, one property

Position lives on the rail, not on any one toast — Toast.position(name) sets data-position and every toast fired afterward opens from the new corner. Toasts already on screen are left exactly where they are. Bottom-right stays the default: nearest the thumb on mobile, out of the way of a right-hand cursor on desktop.

Pick a corner, then fire a couple of toasts from section 01 above — they'll open from wherever the rail is now anchored. Top-* stacks new toasts nearest the header; bottom-* stacks them nearest the thumb. On a phone-width viewport every position spans the full gutter-to-gutter width regardless of left/center/right — a corner-anchored toast on a 360px screen is the whole screen with extra steps, so only the block edge (top or bottom) matters there.

07

How it leaves still tells you why

There is no × anymore — a swipe already exists, and a visible close button next to a retracting fill was two dismiss affordances doing one job. Removing it gave the line real breathing room. The two exits stay deliberately different, so the outcome reads without thinking about it.

Ran out

It collapses, shows its outcome, then goes

Shrinks back to the circle it arrived as, holds the outcome glyph for a beat, fades. Its time simply ended, and it says so on the way out.

Dismissed

It flies, immediately

Swipe it, or focus it and press Delete. It leaves in the direction you pushed, with no collapse — you ended it yourself, so it doesn't perform a countdown that already stopped mattering.

Drag works with a mouse, a finger, or a pen — past a quarter of the pill's width it goes, short of that it springs back and the fill resumes exactly where it paused. Every toast is reachable by Tab; with one focused, Delete, Backspace or Esc dismisses it — the keyboard equivalent of a swipe, not a visible button.

08

Rules

  • bothNeither signal is optional. If a fix makes one quieter, it must move to a property that doesn't compete — it may never simply disappear.
  • oneOne layer carries both required signals: hue for outcome, volume for time. They don't fight because they aren't the same kind of mark.
  • featherThe volume's trailing edge is always a feather, never a cut. On a fully rounded shape a straight border is a foreign object. The feather scales with the volume so nothing is left behind at zero.
  • baseA low base of the same hue sits under the volume and never leaves, so the colour stays readable to the end. No separate bar, badge or marker for identity — those grow geometry that fights the radius.
  • motionThe vocabulary is reveal, lift, spin, draw, sweep, expand — all single-direction, all settling once. No shakes, wiggles or oscillations.
  • sizeOne default width for every toast. Content may push past it, never below it.
  • glyphThe glyph exists only at the threshold — arriving or leaving. It never sits in the body while the toast is open.
  • grep firstBefore adding a colour, a motion or a measure, grep the grammar for it. Nothing returned is usually a decision already made, not a gap to fill. Every deviation in v1 entered by solving inside the component instead of asking what the system already does.
  • no green surface--ok exists once system-wide, as a solid .act fill. It never becomes a wash, a tint or a surface. Success belongs on the exit circle.
  • rail.darkQuantity on a dark surface is already codified: rgba(255,255,255,.12) track, accent fill. The volume follows it.
  • exit = .actThe closing circle reproduces the action button's settled states exactly — solid --ok with a white check, or white with an --err mark.
  • no buttonNo visible × anywhere. Swipe and keyboard cover dismissal; a button next to a retracting fill was redundant.
  • threeThree on screen at most. A fourth expires the oldest through the same exit a timeout uses.
  • pauseThe volume stops retracting while a human is reading, and while the tab is hidden. Resumes, never restarts.
  • button owns successIf a button started the work, the button reports it — .act already owns success.
