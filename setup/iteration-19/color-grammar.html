01

The swap

A palette change touches one block. Every derived token — brand, hover, subtle, chip, focus ring, shimmer — reads through the eleven aliases, so nothing downstream is opened. The current family is true-matcha. 

/* color.css §2 — the entire diff */
[data-palette="true-matcha"]{
  --primary-50:  var(--color-true-matcha-50);
  --primary-100: var(--color-true-matcha-100);
  … 900
  --primary-950: var(--color-true-matcha-950);

  /* contract §1.4 — declared here, not in the mode block */
  --primary-on-light: var(--color-paper-50);
  --primary-on-dark:  var(--color-pure-black-950);
}

What moved

Eleven raw steps. Everything below is derived.

What followed

chip tag
Why the on-colors sit in the family block. A light family — amber, lime, yellow — needs dark text on its 600 step. If that decision lives in the light-mode block instead, it silently claims "white sits on primary" for every family that will ever be mounted, and the first light family drops every filled button to roughly 2:1 with nothing raising a hand. Declaring it per family is what makes the swap safe rather than merely quick.

02

The family contract

A ramp is not a palette until it satisfies six conditions. These are evaluated live against the rendered values, for the family and mode you have selected — so this is a test, not documentation. Flip through the four families and watch it move.

Sunburst passes every contrast anchor and still fails — it sits 1° from --warning-color, so a caution badge and the brand read as the same signal. Persimmon lands 9° from --danger-color. Neither is unusable, but both need an icon or a shape doing the work hue normally does, and that is a decision worth making on purpose.

03

Three tiers, one direction

Values flow one way and never back. A component that reads a ramp step has skipped the contract and will break on the next family — which is why the Tailwind theme exposes the semantic tier only.

  • --blog-tint — nothing is tinted

  • --blog-ink — the text is paper in dark

All nine --blog-* aliases map onto tokens that already exist. §13 of color.css has the find-and-replace.

04

Mix ramp

Five steps, all derived from --foreground-color, so every one inverts for free. The percentages step up in dark because a 10% line on a dark ground reads lighter than a 10% line on a light one. Nothing between the five steps, ever.

Lines carry meaning or they do not exist. A border at the ramp's second step is decorative — it says only "these are two different things", and it is fine that a low-vision reader cannot resolve it. The moment a border means something — focused, selected, invalid, hovered — it jumps to a full-strength token: --foreground-color, --primary-color, --danger-color. There is no middle border.

05

Four grounds

The ground is semantic, not aesthetic — choosing one is choosing what kind of content is inside it. Flip to dark and check that all four remain distinct; that separation is the thing most likely to collapse on a theme change.

--background-color

The document

Prose, sections, navigation, and any card that is content rather than an object. The default — if you are unsure, it is this one.

--surface-color

Human input

Forms, fields, anything typed into or manipulated. One step toward the far end from the page. A form on the page ground reads as a printout; a form on surface reads as a form.

--contrast-background-color

Machine output — inverts

Totals, estimates, breakdowns — the result of a calculation. Never put an input inside it.

Estimated ₹ 180,000

--primary-color-subtle

Asides and marks

Notes, callouts, chips, inline code. How the brand covers area without becoming loud. Never a container for primary content.

The separation law. Contrast panel = numbers. Surface card = human input. Plain row = the call to action. Three jobs, three grounds, never merged into one surface. This is also the rule that breaks quietest in dark mode: if --contrast-background-color and --surface-color resolve to the same step, the estimator result and a raised card render identically and nobody notices until a client does. Going one step deeper in dark measures 1.19:1 against surface and fails; inverting measures 16.10:1, which is what the light mode already had. Contrast is therefore the opposite of the page, not "the dark one".

Focus ring

2px outline at 3px offset, plus the ring wash on fields. One treatment app-wide.

Selection

Drag across this sentence to see it live.

Selection carries text, so it uses the declared on-color rather than assuming white.

Disabled

Opacity, never a grey repaint. A greyed button loses its variant; a faded one keeps it.

Do

One primary object per view. The ghost carries the escape, and the eye has somewhere to land.

Don't

Three primaries is zero emphasis. The brand works because of its ratio on screen, not its hue — and that is true of matcha exactly as it was of persimmon.

09

Depth

Two shadows, one ring, three gradients. A shadow is the foreground at a ramp step, which is why it deepens automatically in dark rather than disappearing.

--shadow-lift

A card responding to the pointer. −28px blur off --shadow-color

--shadow-pop

Floating pill, modal, sticky contact. off --scrim-color

--gradient-placeholder

Unloaded image. Ramp steps only — a placeholder must not compete.

--gradient-shimmer

The brand's subtle step swept across the inert ground. The only animated gradient in the system.

--scrim-nav

ayush.dev

The page ground at 72% plus a 20px blur. The header is the only element permitted to be translucent.

10

The rules

Everything above, compressed. If a review comment needs a citation, it is one of these.

Always

  • Consume the semantic tier — --primary-color, never --primary-600
  • Pick the ground by what is inside it
  • Keep every alpha on the five-step mix ramp
  • Let status stay family-independent
  • Declare on-colors per family
  • Express disabled as opacity
  • Run the contract before adding a family

Never

  • A hex, rgb() or hsl() in a component file
  • A ramp step in a component file
  • A hue in a token name
  • A second copy of the dark values
  • More than one primary action in a view
  • A middle-strength border
  • An input inside a contrast panel, or a total inside a surface card
  • A third shadow or a fourth gradient

Before you open a PR

  1. Zero colour literals and zero ramp steps in the component file?
  2. Every alpha from the five mix-ramp tokens?
  3. Exactly one .btn-primary in the view?
  4. Every text–ground pair legal in both modes?
  5. Contrast panel still distinct from surface in dark?
  6. Focus outline present and unmodified?
  7. Only --shadow-lift and --shadow-pop?
  8. Disabled expressed as opacity?
  9. Checked under forced-colors: active and in print?
  10. Contract still green after any theme change?
