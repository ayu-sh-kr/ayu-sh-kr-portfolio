Design grammar · 03

Nine triggers. Sixteen verbs. No drift.

Every interactive behaviour on this site is one of nine families, and every one of them is on this page — live, in every variant it comes in, with the value it uses and where it actually appears.

01

Pointer response

Eight tokens, and they never carry information — a third of the people here have no pointer at all. Hover each tile.

Where each one goes

Lift — every .btn, .act, card, row, toast. Deep lift — only a card that is itself the link: tiers, featured, colophon doors. Ink-up — nav, TOC, ghost borders, footer links. Wash — routes and quick-help; the quiet variant for list rows that must not shout. Nudge — any row with a trailing .arr. Deepen — filled buttons only. Press — coarse pointers and the tilt card.

Lift XOR nudge. A container lifts, or a mark inside it travels — never both, or it reads as two objects coming apart.

Hover is garnish

Do

Border inks up, arrow nudges. One parent state, one child move.

Don't

Row lifts and the arrow travels. Two motions, one point.

02

Focus and keyboard

One ring — 2px solid var(--accent) — and three offsets, chosen by what the ring has to clear. Press the buttons to show each, or tab through them.

Offset 3px

outline-offset:3px

Buttons, .act, toast, nav links — anywhere there is open paper to spend.

Offset 2px

outline-offset:2px

Fields, choice cards, drop zones — packed into a grid, where 3px touches a neighbour.

Offset −2px

outline-offset:-2px

FAQ summary rows and list rows with no padding to spare. The ring goes inside and takes a small radius.

Key contracts

  • TabReaches everything, including each live toast, in document order.
  • EscCancels a dialog, dismisses a focused toast, ignored in flight.
  • DeleteDismisses the focused toast — the keyboard swipe.
  • EnterThe default action, never the destructive one.
  • SpaceToggles a choice card or chip — both are real buttons.

Also in this family

Focus-within — a container mirrors its own hover when anything inside takes focus, so a keyboard user gets the same emphasis. Focus return — closing an overlay puts focus back on the opener. Trap and inert — while a blocking layer is open the rest of the page is unreachable, by the platform's top layer rather than a simulation.

A risk-tone dialog never focuses its confirm button — it opens on cancel, so a reflexive Enter is safe. Never remove an outline without replacing it with the ring; a background change is invisible to someone tabbing fast.

03

Scroll response

Five of these are already running while you read: the rail at the top of the window, the hairline under the header, every block fading up once, and the TOC marking where you are.

One number, three consumers. No element inside a pin runs an animation of its own — and no .reveal ever sits in a stage, because it fires on entry and then the scrub takes it back out from under itself.

One passive scroll listener, one rAF, one render function per page. Everything scroll-driven above — the two rails, the hairline, the pin — reads from the same frame. Reading layout inside a raw scroll handler is what makes a page feel heavy on a phone.

04

Selection and disclosure

Selection is a state, so it is drawn with colour and a mark and never with motion. Disclosure is a change of extent, so it is drawn with measure and never with a fade alone.

Choice card · single

border + tint + dot

Selected owns colour, hover owns transform. They never touch the same property, so they coexist with no rule about which wins — hover a selected card and it still lifts.

Chip toggle · multi

background → --ink

Inverts to solid ink. Used where several answers are legal at once and the options are short enough to read as a set.

Disclosure

rotate(45deg) · .3s

Why native details?+

It brings the open state into the DOM, keyboard operation, and find-in-page expansion for nothing. Every hand-rolled accordion here was replaced by one.

Why a fixed ceiling on panels?+

auto is not an animatable length, so a transition to it snaps.

The + rotates into a cross. It never swaps glyph — a swap reads as two icons rather than one hinge.

Panel reveal & branch

max-block-size .45s

A panel, not an accordion

Trigger stays put, carries aria-expanded, points at the panel with aria-controls. Rises 6px so the eye follows it out.

A branch replaces the fieldset beneath it — the outgoing set is removed, not collapsed, and the incoming one fades up.

05

Ingest

Handing the system something. The one place the pill grammar is allowed to break, because a drop target has to read as an area rather than a control.

Field

--fs-field · 16px coarse

Steps to 16px under @media (pointer: coarse). Anything smaller makes iOS Safari zoom the viewport on focus — a layout bug in a typography costume.

Drop zone

.dragging · scale(1.01)

    The only dashed non-element in the system. Drag-over changes four things at once — the one place that is right, because the human is holding something.

    Accepted & submit

    popIn .3s

    • brief-v3.pdf248 KB

    Chips pop in on accept; removal is immediate with no exit animation — it is gone because you removed it. Submit hands off to family 06; a form never invents its own pending state.

    06

    Action lifecycle

    Five states via data-state, and one architectural line: state lives in the machine, and the button only reflects what was published.

    idle

    No glyph node at all — the label never shifts to make room for an empty box.

    pending

    Disabled, hover suppressed, cursor:progress. Indeterminate, because the duration genuinely is.

    success

    The only sanctioned green in the system. The check draws along its own stroke.

    error

    Inverts rather than fills, so failure doesn't shout at the same volume as success.

    Run it

    idle

    Static .btn and stateful .act are different components and never substitute. Auto-return: 2200ms from success, 2600ms from error, 12s pending timeout.

    Route progress

    ceiling .90 · tick 90ms

    Watch the top of the window. Ref-counted, decelerating toward a ceiling it cannot cross without a completion. Turns --err on failure.

    Skeleton

    sweep 1.4s · stagger 80ms

    Predicts the real layout exactly — same measure, same rows. A skeleton that guesses wrong is worse than a spinner.

    Live recomputation

    Output that follows input without a submit. The whole difficulty is attribution — which number moved, and because of what.

    Only the row that changed flashes — a 16% accent wash decaying to transparent. Flashing the whole list means flashing nothing.

    Dark panel · numbers

    Dark panel is numbers, white card is human input, plain row is the CTA. Established on the coffee page and not to be regressed. Every digit is tabular-nums, and the whole change is announced through one aria-live region, never one per number.

    08

    Transient feedback

    The system speaking without being asked. Toast is an ink pill, cornered, and passes; the dialog is a white card, centred, and blocks — opposites on every axis, deliberately.

    Anatomy · held open

    hue = outcome · volume = time left

    Draft saved×3
    one layer, two signals feathered trailing edge base tint never leaves no visible ×

    Hue says what happened, length says how long you have. The trailing edge is always a feather, never a cut — on a fully rounded shape a straight border is a foreign object — and the feather scales with the volume so nothing is left at zero.

    Fire one

    max 3 · coalesce by id

    The same event twice is not two toasts — the live pill refills and ticks a tabular ×n. A fourth retires the oldest through the timeout exit, because that is exactly what happened to it.

    Two exits

    collapse vs. fly

    Ran out — collapses back to the circle it arrived as, holds the outcome glyph a beat, fades.
    Dismissed — Esc or Delete on a focused pill, and it just goes.

    They stay different on purpose: you ended it yourself, so it doesn't perform a countdown that stopped mattering. It also pauses while you read and while the tab is hidden — resuming, never restarting.

    Confirm & ping

    1400ms · ping 2s

    Replies within a day

    Small things get no toast — a control briefly becomes its own receipt. The ping is the one infinite animation that isn't reporting work, and only ever on a live state.

    If a button started the work, the button reports it. A success toast next to an .act that already went green is the same fact twice. Toast is for outcomes whose trigger is off-screen or already gone.

    10

    Timing and easing

    Six durations and one spatial curve. Two others exist and are attached to a specific behaviour — they may not be borrowed.

    One press, four durations

    var(--ease)

    .2s
    .25s
    .35s
    .7s

    Anything a human does deliberately gets .25s or less — slower than that reads as latency rather than motion. The .7s bar is the reveal, and it is the ceiling.

    The curves

    .2,.8,.2,1

    ■ .2,.8,.2,1 — every movement.
    ▫ .2,.9,.25,1 — press only. ▫ .35,0,.25,1 — skeleton sweep only.

    Opacity gets ease, movement gets the curve, per-frame values get linear. That is the whole rule. If a new easing function feels necessary, the duration is usually what's wrong.

    What each duration is for

    .12s a press, or a per-frame value, linear · .2s colour only — no distance to travel, no settle · .25s the default move: any transform under ~8px · .3–.35s a glyph drawing itself, a mark rotating, a deep lift · .42–.55s a measure changing: a width grown to, a panel to a ceiling · .7s reveal on scroll, once per element, the ceiling.

    The verb list

    Every motion in the app is one of these sixteen, all single-direction, all settling exactly once. Press one to play it.

    Not in the vocabulary

    shakewigglebounceelastic overshootpulse oscillateparallax on texttypewritercursor follower confettimarqueeflip3D carouselscroll-jack

    Everything here either repeats without resolving, or moves in two directions to make one point. Both read as decoration — and decoration in a portfolio for engineering work is a claim about judgement.

    12

    Compounding

    Almost every interaction bug in this codebase was two correct behaviours running at once.

    • one motionOne motion idea per section. Competing animations, especially inside a pin, kill clarity faster than no animation at all.
    • parent + oneA container may change state while one child moves. Never two children.
    • lift xor nudgeAn element lifts, or a mark inside it travels. Not both.
    • state > hoverSelection owns colour, hover owns transform — they never touch the same property.
    • pending winsAn in-flight action suppresses its own hover and disables itself. A button that still lifts is lying about being available.
    • one blockerNever two blocking layers. A dialog that needs a second question is a form, and a form belongs on a page.
    • one reporterOne outcome, one report. If the button went green, no toast follows.
    • no reveal in pinA .reveal never sits inside a pinned stage.
    • measure onceOnly floating chrome measures itself to grow. Nothing in flow changes its own width on hover.
    • one listenerOne passive scroll listener, one rAF, one render function per page.
    • grep firstBefore adding a duration, a curve or a verb, grep the grammar for it. Nothing returned is usually a decision already made, not a gap to fill.

    13

    Reduced motion

    The contract is one sentence: reduced motion removes motion, never information — which is also why nothing in this document carries meaning in its movement alone.

    Simulate it

    data-rm="on"

    Applies the same declarations the media query applies, to the same targets. Scroll back up with it on — everything stays legible, the pin unpins into normal flow, and every verb above lands on a still frame that was designed.

    A disabled animation must not become a frozen artefact. The spinner loses rotation and gains a solid ring at 50%; the check and cross render fully drawn; the ping ring goes to opacity:0 rather than sitting expanded forever.

    What changes

    Transitions drop to .001ms, not none, so state changes still commit and transitionend still fires. Reveals start visible and untranslated. Pins collapse to auto and become static with normal section padding. Loops stop on an explicit resting style. Smooth scroll returns to auto.

    The toggle is a review aid, never a persisted preference — and it must stay a duplicate of the media query. A rule added to one goes into the other in the same commit.

    14

    Never

    Each of these entered the codebase at least once and had to be taken back out.

    • hover-onlyNever make hover the only way to discover a control, a label or a destination.
    • motion-onlyNever carry information in movement alone.
    • allNever transition:all — it animates properties you didn't choose. Ten instances remain in the app and are being replaced with explicit lists.
    • new curveNever a fourth easing function, and never borrow the press or sweep curve.
    • max-height:noneNever transition to none or auto. It snaps.
    • outline:0Never remove a focus outline without replacing it with the system ring.
    • infiniteNever an infinite animation that isn't reporting live work — spinner, sweep, one status ping.
    • jackNever intercept scroll, hijack a wheel event, or parallax body text.
    • z-45Never a bare integer z-index. Needing a level between two tokens means the scheme is wrong.
    • layout on hoverNever change an in-flow element's size on hover.
    • re-hideNever let a reveal run twice.
    • greenNever spend --ok outside a settled .act and the toast exit circle.
    • importedNever import a behaviour because it is common elsewhere. Every one here derives from this page's own visual language.

    15

    Ship checklist

    No interactive behaviour goes out without all of these.

    1. The behaviour belongs to exactly one of the nine families
    2. Every hover has a :focus-visible equivalent
    3. Reachable and operable by keyboard alone, in document order
    4. No information carried by motion alone
    5. Every duration on the six-step scale; every spatial curve var(--ease)
    6. Every verb already in the sixteen
    7. One motion idea in the section it lives in
    8. State published by a machine, reflected by the UI — never decided by it
    9. Async states cannot be stranded: timeout, cancel path, settled outcome
    10. Announced through one aria-live region, not many
    11. Scroll work passive, rAF-throttled, sharing the page's single frame
    12. Checked with reduced motion on: no frozen artefacts, nothing lost
    13. Checked with a keyboard, a touch screen, and at 200% browser zoom
    14. Checked at 320px, 768px, 1440px, and on a landscape phone near 700×360
