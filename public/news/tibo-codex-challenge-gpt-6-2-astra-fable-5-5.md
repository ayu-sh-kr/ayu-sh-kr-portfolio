# Tibo’s 28-day Codex challenge ends as fans wait for the next model

For 28 days, Codex users had a reason to check Tibo’s feed: a product improvement, a usage reset, or both. As the final days approached, some hoped for something bigger—a new OpenAI model to answer Claude Opus 5.5.

The challenge ended on **November 1 with a banked reset**, not a frontier-model announcement. Around the same time, screenshots of an unfamiliar `gpt-6.2-astra` label appeared in usage histories. Across the rivalry, rumors of Claude Fable 5.5 were circulating too.

Neither name is a confirmed public launch. The gap between that evidence and the excitement around it tells its own story about how AI releases are now followed.

## A promise that became a daily event

On October 4, OpenAI’s Thibault “Tibo” Sottiaux [promised](https://codexreset.ai/28-day-challenge/) that for the next 28 days the team would either ship an improvement relevant to most Codex and ChatGPT Work users or give a full usage reset. A reset refreshes a covered allowance; a **banked reset** can be saved and redeemed later, subject to its terms.

The updates included faster responses, instant steering, a larger default context window and, on October 29, [higher usage limits for Pro tiers](https://nepentheplay.com/games/codex-28/). The final two days brought banked resets. Tibo had teased those resets with “do with this what you may,” leaving room for people to imagine what they might spend them on.

That anticipation was not the same as a promise of a model. The original commitment was to improvements or resets, and the final [Day 28 post](https://x.com/thsottiaux/status/2117035794678546764) announced the second banked reset.

Still, the daily format gave small changes the rhythm of a release calendar. Independent trackers appeared; users compared updates, waited for resets and discussed what might close the series. It was product communication with a marketing effect: each day brought people back to see what happened next. That effect is visible in the community activity, even if OpenAI has not described it as the campaign’s purpose.

## Gratitude, disappointment and an unfinished wish

The response was mixed. In one [X discussion](https://xitter.cf/TokenGremlin/status/2116820985873571993), Token Gremlin said they appreciated the updates but felt something was unfinished, later naming a model above the Terra tier as something they wanted. Replies ranged from appreciation for the resets and shipped changes to disappointment that no bigger model arrived.

Those replies are **individual reactions, not a representative survey**. They show why the same month could feel generous to one user and underwhelming to another: a fresh allowance helps someone hitting limits today, while a developer waiting for a stronger coding model is looking for a different kind of improvement.

The comparison hanging over that wait was real. Anthropic launched [Claude Opus 5.5](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-opus-5-5) on September 22. Anthropic says it matches GPT-6 Astra on Terminal-Bench 4.0 for roughly 40% of the cost in its evaluation; that is the company’s reported comparison for a specific benchmark and settings, not proof that one model wins every task.

## The names in the usage logs

On November 1, users shared [screenshots](https://enjoyagentic.com/blog/gpt-6-2-astra/) showing `gpt-6.2-astra` alongside `gpt-6-astra` in usage history, while their model selector still showed Astra. One shared response snippet also carried the name. The screenshots have not been independently authenticated against the accounts, and the plan tier is not established for all of them; there is **no verified basis to say the label is exclusive to $500 subscribers**.

A label could reflect a test, a routing or reporting change, or something else. It does not establish the model’s capabilities, public availability or release date. As checked on November 2, [OpenAI’s model catalogue](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models) did not list GPT-6.2 Astra.

The Fable story has a similar boundary. A [LiteLLM change](https://github.com/BerriAI/litellm/pull/46201) briefly added an Azure entry named `claude-fable-5-5` in October; a later removal and [independent tracking](https://cellcog.ai/blog/fable-5-5-release-date/) complicate any reading of that entry as a release. As of November 2, Anthropic’s [model documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview) lists **Fable 5.1**, alongside the released Opus 5.5. Fable 5.5 has not been announced there, and no performance comparison with it can be made from a catalog label.

## Why the timing attracts attention

There is more at stake than a leaderboard. Anthropic [filed for an IPO on October 26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026067168/anthropicpbc-sx1.htm), while OpenAI has said it does not plan to go public in 2026. In September, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/business/anthropic-considers-releasing-new-ai-model-ahead-ipo-sources-say-2026-09-19/) that Anthropic was considering a model release amid competitive pressure from Astra and an expected IPO. That report preceded Opus 5.5; it did not establish a Fable 5.5 release or a launch timed to the filing.

The tension between competition and safety also runs through Anthropic’s [responses to open-weight rivals](/news/anthropic-open-weight-models-security-timeline/). There are reasons a model may take longer than an online countdown suggests. Reuters [reported from the IPO filing](https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/anthropic-discloses-month-long-training-pause-ai-security-breaches-ipo-filing-2026-10-26/) that Anthropic had paused pre-training of a next-generation model for more than a month to strengthen protections against theft. The disclosure does not identify that model as Fable 5.5 or explain OpenAI’s plans.

The challenge gave users a month of tangible changes and a daily story to follow. It also showed how quickly a countdown, a pair of saved resets and an unexplained model name can become an anticipated launch. For now, the next chapter is still waiting for an announcement—and for users to test whatever actually ships.
