# Asonix brings App Store keyword research and AI visibility into one tool

Publishing an iPhone app does not reveal which words prospective users will search for, or which markets offer room for it to be discovered. **Asonix**, launched publicly by Guilherme Rizzo, brings keyword demand, competing apps, advertising activity and recommendation checks into a browser-based research tool.

Its focus is **App Store optimization**, usually shortened to ASO: improving how an app is found and presented in the store. The product also tracks whether ChatGPT recommends an app, adding another discovery channel to research that traditionally concentrates on search rankings.

## The question is which search is worth targeting

Apple [describes App Store ranking](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/search/) as a combination of text relevance and user behavior, including downloads, ratings and reviews. Accurate titles and keywords matter, but popular words also attract competition. A small app can appear relevant to “productivity” while remaining difficult to find among established products.

[Asonix](https://asonix.io/) lets a developer compare a keyword’s popularity, ranking difficulty and relevance to the app, then see which products already appear. Its opportunity score combines demand and competition; it is the vendor’s estimate, rather than an Apple ranking rule.

For a study timer, that turns a vague ambition to reach more users into researchable options. You can compare broad terms with more specific study-related phrases, examine the competing listings and decide which terms accurately describe the product. A high opportunity score is only useful when the app serves that search intent.

The product covers **175 Apple storefronts**, including iPhone, iPad and Mac apps. Country comparisons can expose a different competitive situation for the same kind of app. Translation helps a developer read unfamiliar keywords, although a translated phrase still needs local-language judgment before it becomes listing copy.

## Observed data and estimated demand sit side by side

The site explains that rankings and ratings come from App Store observations, advertiser information from observed search ads, and Meta creatives from Meta’s Ad Library. It also supplies download and revenue estimates, which it labels approximate.

Keyword popularity needs particular care. Asonix uses Apple Search Ads values where available, but estimates demand where Apple does not provide a useful score or does not operate the ad product. A number in every country does not mean Apple supplied every number.

The practical distinction is between **seeing where an app appeared** and **estimating how many people might want it**. Both can help research, but neither proves a future download result. The product does not publish an independently validated uplift benchmark establishing that its keyword scores cause a particular increase in installs.

## AI visibility checks a different route to discovery

Asonix’s AI Visibility feature runs questions against ChatGPT weekly and records mentions, recommendations, competing apps and supporting sources. It can help show whether an app appears for the kinds of questions a potential user asks.

That is a sampled set of prompts and model responses. It should not be read as a complete measurement of every user’s recommendations, or as a stable search rank. The useful evidence is the recorded question, answer and source trail, followed over time under comparable conditions.

The product also provides an **MCP server**, connecting this research to assistants such as Claude Code, Codex or Cursor. An agent can query keyword and competitor data and add keywords to a tracking workspace. Asonix remains the data service; the assistant provides another way to use it.

## Who built it, price and alternatives

Rizzo also makes **CSS Scan, CSS Pro and Drawings Alive**. His [company account](https://asonix.io/about) dates Asonix’s founding to April 2026 and describes it as a product of Optimistic Invent, LLC. The launch post is a public introduction to that product, rather than evidence that development began this week.

The listed subscription is **$99 a year**, with unlimited apps and keywords and no free plan. It works from public store data and does not require access to the developer’s App Store Connect account. It is a commercial hosted service; no downloadable source-code license is offered in the product documentation.

[Appfigures](https://help.appfigures.com/en/article/appfigures-app-intelligence-gn1nz5/) and [AppTweak](https://developers.apptweak.com/reference/keyword-search) are alternatives to compare for broader app intelligence and Google Play coverage. [Astro](https://tryastro.app/) is another option for App Store keyword work, with a Mac-app workflow. Apple’s own App Store Connect analytics remains important for checking actual impressions, conversion and downloads after a listing change.

[Neeed’s independent directory entry](https://neeed.directory/products/asonix) describes the same keyword and competitor focus, but does not establish a measured growth result. That is the appropriate boundary for a new product’s coverage: features can be documented before their commercial impact is independently demonstrated.

For the study timer, the useful loop is to identify a relevant opportunity, publish accurate listing changes and observe whether discovery improves. Asonix brings several inputs to that decision together. The app’s own analytics still have to show whether the decision worked.
