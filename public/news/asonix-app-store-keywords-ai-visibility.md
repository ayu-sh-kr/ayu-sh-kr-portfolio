# Asonix combines App Store keyword research with ChatGPT visibility

**Asonix brings App Store keyword research and ChatGPT recommendation checks into one service**, reflecting two ways people now discover apps: searching a store and asking an assistant.

Created by **Guilherme Rizzo**, who also makes CSS Scan, the product combines keyword demand, competing listings and advertising activity. Its App Store research covers **175 storefronts**, including iPhone, iPad and Mac apps.

## From search terms to discovery patterns

App Store optimization depends on understanding how people search and which listings appear for those terms. Asonix combines keyword popularity, ranking difficulty and relevance into an opportunity score, helping organize that research across markets.

The figures come from different sources. Rankings and ratings are observed in the store. Keyword popularity uses Apple Search Ads values where available, with estimates filling gaps. Download and revenue figures are also approximate. The opportunity score is Asonix’s interpretation of those inputs, rather than an Apple ranking rule.

Its **AI Visibility** feature extends the research to ChatGPT. Weekly checks record whether an app appears in responses, which other apps are recommended and what sources support the answers. Those observations capture a sample of prompts and responses; they do not represent every user’s recommendations.

## Research inputs, rather than a growth forecast

Asonix lists a **$99 annual subscription**, with unlimited apps and keywords and no free plan. It works from public store data without requiring App Store Connect access. An MCP server also makes the research available through assistants such as Claude Code, Codex and Cursor.

Bringing these signals together makes app discovery easier to examine across search and conversational recommendations. Whether changes informed by that research produce more installs is a separate question, answered by the app’s own impressions, conversion and download data. Asonix provides evidence for the decision; the subsequent results establish whether it helped.
