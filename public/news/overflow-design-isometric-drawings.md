# Overflow Design refreshes its isometric illustration library

**Overflow Design has refreshed its isometric library**, giving designers a way to set colors and preview drawings on light or dark backgrounds before copying them. Creator Zafar Ismatullaev says the collection contains **249 drawings**.

The catalogue spans objects and scenes: servers and databases for technical diagrams, vehicles and buildings for logistics, and interface elements for product pages. The shared perspective and line style make them useful together when a page needs more depth than a flat icon.

The announcement shows the library’s category browser, appearance controls and drawing grid:

![Overflow Design's isometric library, showing categories, appearance controls and illustrations of logistics equipment](/news/assets/overflow-design-isometric-drawings/overflow-interface.jpg)

## From a drawing to a working interface

A team building a delivery dashboard could choose a truck, adjust its colors to match the product and check it against both themes. Overflow also offers a Figma plugin and an MCP server for searching its assets from compatible coding agents. The wider catalogue includes hand-drawn icons and illustrations for office, Notion and ecommerce contexts.

The direction is toward a **reusable visual system**: select a consistent drawing, adapt it to a palette and bring it into a design or code workflow. The paid isometric collection also includes animated versions that can play automatically or respond to a pointer.

## The trade-off

Overflow lists **71 isometric stills as free**; the remaining drawings and animations require a license. Commercial use is allowed, but redistribution and template use have specific restrictions, so check the [license](https://www.overflow.design/license/) before shipping.

A shared style saves illustration work, but it can also make products look alike. Color controls help a drawing fit a brand; they cannot make a stock scene specific to the product. For the delivery dashboard, the library is a useful starting point—provided the selected scene actually communicates the workflow.
