# CrowdStrike agrees to buy Zscaler for $43.9 billion

CrowdStrike’s Falcon can already tell Zscaler whether a laptop meets a company’s security requirements before it connects to an internal app. On November 23, [CrowdStrike announced an agreement to buy Zscaler](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/press-releases/crowdstrike-to-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero-trust-to-the-falcon-platform/) in an **all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion**. The deal builds on that partnership: the companies want to bring the tools, the data they collect and the contracts customers use to buy them together.

| Deal term | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Enterprise value | About $43.9 billion, based on CrowdStrike’s November 20 closing share price |
| Payment | 1.135 CrowdStrike shares per Zscaler share |
| Target close | Second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal 2028, subject to regulatory and Zscaler shareholder approvals and other closing conditions |

## What already works together

**CrowdStrike Falcon** monitors laptops, servers and workloads for suspicious activity. **Zscaler** controls the connections people and devices make to apps and the internet. Its zero-trust approach checks whether a request meets access rules instead of trusting a device simply because it’s on the corporate network.

The [existing device-check integration](https://help.zscaler.com/zscaler-technology-partners/zscaler-and-crowdstrike-deployment-guide) shows how those jobs fit together. Falcon assesses a device’s security status; Zscaler can use that assessment to allow or block access to a private app. The companies report **more than 2,000 shared customers and 16 integrations**.

## What the deal could change

The two companies say they will **operate independently until closing**. Afterward, CrowdStrike plans to let customers of either company add the other’s products through one Falcon Flex contract. It also plans to bring endpoint and network activity into a shared data layer, so teams can investigate what happened on a device alongside the connections it made.

For teams already using both products, the security checks described above are available through the partnership today. The practical change to watch for after closing is whether they can buy both under one contract and move between device and network evidence in the same investigation.
