[Indiknots](https://www.indiknots.com/) brings handcrafted rugs into contemporary homes. The website needed to carry the material warmth and craft behind the collections while staying practical for someone furnishing a real room.

![Indiknots homepage showing a new-season hand-knotted rug in a living room](/showcases/indiknots/indiknots-hero.png)

## One design grammar, used everywhere

The site is built around a single source of truth for layout, typography, and color. Shared rules keep the experience coherent from navigation to collection pages, while leaving room for the rugs, rooms, and editorial moments to feel distinct.

## Make exploration feel natural

The catalog is structured around the decisions customers already make: the room they are styling, the purpose a rug needs to serve, and the collection that catches their eye. That keeps discovery simple without reducing a considered product to a search result.

## Let the visuals do the explaining

The experience communicates visually before it explains itself in prose. Large product imagery, room-scale compositions, and a calm rhythm let visitors understand the texture, scale, and mood of a rug before they reach the product detail.

Text on imagery was treated as part of the composition, not a label placed on top of a photograph. Its contrast, position, and measure shift to preserve the image while giving each collection a clear entry point.

<showcase-aside kind="note">A retail site for crafted objects should feel tactile without making the path to purchase feel precious or slow.</showcase-aside>

## Commerce is the next layer

The live experience supports browsing, customization, trade enquiries, and global shipping information in one coherent journey. Shop and payment integration are work in progress; the same design grammar will carry through checkout so commerce feels like a natural continuation of discovery.
